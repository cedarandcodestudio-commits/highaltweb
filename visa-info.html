Visa & entry info.

Entry rules vary by passport, destination, and trip length, and they change often. Here's how to stay ahead of them.

Start with your passport.

Many countries require your passport to be valid for at least six months beyond your travel dates and to have blank pages for stamps. Check your expiration date before you book anything, and renew early if you're close.

Check your destination.

Requirements depend on the passport you hold. Always confirm with official government sources:

  • U.S. citizens: travel.state.gov has entry requirements and advisories for every country.
  • Europe (Schengen Area): the EU is introducing new digital entry systems for visitors. See the official Travel to Europe site for current status.
  • United Kingdom: many visitors, including U.S. citizens, now need an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA). Apply only through gov.uk/eta.
  • Everywhere else: use the destination country's official embassy or immigration website.

Our top tips.

  • Apply for visas and e-authorizations through official government sites only. Third-party sites often charge extra fees.
  • Leave plenty of time. Some visas take weeks to process.
  • Check transit rules too, since some countries require a visa even for a layover.
  • Enroll in your government's traveler program (for U.S. citizens, STEP) for safety alerts.

This page is general guidance, not legal or immigration advice. Rules change frequently, so always confirm requirements with official sources before you travel. Your advisor will flag entry requirements for your specific itinerary.
